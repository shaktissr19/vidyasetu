## Purpose

This concept follows Force and Effects of Force. It helps learners classify forces by whether direct physical contact is required and then distinguish familiar examples: muscular force, friction, magnetic force, electrostatic force and gravitational force.

## VidyaSetu learning journey

- **SEE:** compare pushing a book, a magnet attracting a steel object, and a falling ball.
- **UNDERSTAND:** classify contact vs non-contact forces and connect each type to an interaction.
- **DO:** safe observation stations using a toy block, a large classroom magnet, paper clips, a plastic comb and tissue bits.
- **PRACTISE:** 12 bilingual questions with reasoning and misconception repair.
- **APPLY:** classify forces in a school-day scenario and explain why the classification is valid.
- **REVISE:** bilingual recap + mastery check.

## Core misconceptions addressed

1. Every force requires objects to touch.
2. Magnetic force always attracts.
3. Gravity acts only on falling objects.
4. Friction is a non-contact force.
5. Muscular force and contact force mean exactly the same thing.
