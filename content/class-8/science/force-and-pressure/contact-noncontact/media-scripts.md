## 1. Concept video — “Contact or non-contact? Follow the interaction”

Target: 5–6 minutes

Format: presenter + object demonstrations + simple animation overlays.

### Scene 1 — Hook (0:00–0:35)

Visual sequence:
- hand pushes a book,
- magnet moves a paper clip across a small gap,
- ball is released and falls.

English narration:
“Three forces. But only one of these needs direct touch. That difference gives us a powerful way to classify forces.”

Hindi narration:
“तीन बल। लेकिन इनमें से केवल एक के लिए सीधा स्पर्श जरूरी है। इसी अंतर से हम बलों का उपयोगी वर्गीकरण कर सकते हैं।”

On-screen prompt:
**Does the force need direct contact? / क्या बल के लिए सीधा संपर्क जरूरी है?**

### Scene 2 — Contact-force idea (0:35–1:35)

Show:
- pushing chair,
- pulling bag,
- shoe gripping floor,
- block sliding on a table.

Explain:
- direct physical contact,
- muscular force as an example,
- friction as another example,
- contact force is the larger category.

Misconception card:
“Muscular force = every contact force” → **No**.

### Scene 3 — Non-contact-force idea (1:35–3:15)

Magnetic demo:
- large classroom magnet approaches paper clip without touching.

Electrostatic demo:
- charged plastic comb near tissue pieces.

Gravity visual:
- release ball;
- cut to stationary book on desk.

Explain:
- magnetic,
- electrostatic,
- gravitational.

Misconception cards:
- “Magnetic force always attracts” → show two magnets repelling.
- “Gravity acts only while falling” → show book on desk with downward gravity arrow and upward support arrow.

### Scene 4 — Classification routine (3:15–4:20)

Animated decision path:

1. Which objects interact?
2. Is direct touch required for this force?
3. Contact or non-contact?
4. Which force type?

Rapid examples:
- cyclist braking,
- magnet + pin,
- falling fruit,
- charged ruler + paper,
- student pushing desk.

### Scene 5 — Several forces at once (4:20–5:10)

Visual:
student holds steel paper clip near magnet.

Overlay arrows:
- magnetic attraction,
- gravity,
- contact force from fingers.

Narration:
“Contact and non-contact forces are not enemies. They can act on the same object at the same time.”

### Scene 6 — Check understanding (5:10–5:40)

Question:
“A football is kicked and then moves through the air. Which interaction is contact, and which important interaction is non-contact?”

Pause 5 seconds.

Reveal:
- kick → contact,
- gravity → non-contact.

### Accessibility

- English and Hindi subtitle tracks.
- Full transcript in both languages.
- Do not communicate classification by colour alone.
- Narrate every important arrow/diagram.
- Keep embedded text minimal so language tracks can reuse visuals.

## 2. Practical video — “Four force observation stations”

Target: 4–5 minutes

Safety: **GREEN classroom activity**, with magnet supervision note.

### Safety card

Use a large classroom magnet. Avoid tiny loose magnets. Keep magnets away from sensitive electronics and relevant medical devices. No sharp objects, flame, chemicals or mains electricity.

### Materials

- toy block/eraser,
- smooth table,
- rough cloth/mat,
- large bar magnet,
- steel paper clips,
- plastic comb,
- dry tissue bits,
- lightweight ball.

### Station A — Push

Action:
push block with hand.

Observation prompt:
“Did the force require contact?”

Answer:
Yes → contact force.

### Station B — Friction

Action:
slide block over smooth and rough surfaces.

Observation prompt:
“Are the surfaces touching while friction acts?”

Answer:
Yes → friction is contact force.

Teaching note:
do not overclaim that roughness alone determines friction in every situation.

### Station C — Magnet

Action:
bring magnet near paper clip without touching.

Observation prompt:
“When did the paper clip begin to move?”

Answer:
before touch → evidence of non-contact magnetic interaction.

### Station D — Electrostatic

Action:
rub dry plastic comb through dry hair or suitable dry fabric, approach tissue bits.

Observation prompt:
“Can movement begin before direct touch?”

Answer:
yes → electrostatic non-contact interaction.

### Gravity close

Release lightweight ball.

Question:
“Does Earth need to touch the ball directly to attract it?”

Answer:
No → gravitational force is non-contact.

### Production rule

Film close-ups that clearly preserve a visible gap before movement in magnetic/electrostatic demonstrations.

## 3. Revision audio — English

Target: 2.5–3 minutes

“Here is the fastest way to remember contact and non-contact forces. Start by asking one question: does this particular force require the interacting objects to touch?

If direct touch is required, it is a contact force. A hand pushing a chair is a contact interaction. Muscular force is one example. Friction is another. Friction acts between surfaces that are in contact and often opposes relative motion or the tendency of relative motion.

If direct touch is not required, the force can be non-contact. At this level, remember three important examples: magnetic force, electrostatic force and gravitational force.

A magnet can affect a suitable object across a small gap. Magnetic interactions can attract or repel, so do not memorise the wrong idea that magnets only attract.

A charged object can also produce an electrostatic attraction or repulsion without direct touch. The familiar charged-comb and tissue demonstration helps us see this.

Gravity is another non-contact force. Earth attracts a dropped ball, but gravity does not disappear when an object is stationary. A book resting on a desk still experiences gravity; the desk also provides a contact support force.

That last example gives us one more important idea: several forces can act on the same object at the same time, and they do not all have to belong to the same category.

So remember the decision rule: identify the interacting objects, ask whether direct contact is required, classify the force, and then name the interaction. Contact: muscular force, friction. Non-contact: magnetic, electrostatic, gravitational. Then use evidence, not memorisation, to justify your answer.”

## 4. Revision audio — Hindi

Target: 2.5–3 minutes

“संपर्क और असंपर्क बल याद रखने का सबसे आसान तरीका एक सवाल से शुरू होता है: क्या इस बल के लिए परस्पर क्रिया करने वाली वस्तुओं का सीधे छूना जरूरी है?

अगर सीधा स्पर्श जरूरी है, तो वह संपर्क बल है। हाथ से कुर्सी धकेलना संपर्क परस्पर क्रिया है। पेशीय बल इसका एक उदाहरण है। घर्षण दूसरा उदाहरण है। घर्षण संपर्क में आई सतहों के बीच काम करता है और अक्सर सापेक्ष गति या उसकी प्रवृत्ति का विरोध करता है।

अगर सीधे स्पर्श की आवश्यकता नहीं है, तो बल असंपर्क हो सकता है। इस स्तर पर तीन महत्वपूर्ण उदाहरण याद रखें: चुंबकीय बल, स्थिरवैद्युत बल और गुरुत्वाकर्षण बल।

चुंबक थोड़ी दूरी से उपयुक्त वस्तु को प्रभावित कर सकता है। चुंबकीय परस्पर क्रिया आकर्षित या प्रतिकर्षित दोनों कर सकती है, इसलिए यह गलत विचार न रखें कि चुंबक केवल आकर्षित करते हैं।

आवेशित वस्तु बिना सीधे स्पर्श के स्थिरवैद्युत आकर्षण या प्रतिकर्षण कर सकती है। आवेशित कंघे और टिश्यू का उदाहरण इसे समझने में मदद करता है।

गुरुत्वाकर्षण भी असंपर्क बल है। पृथ्वी छोड़ी हुई गेंद को आकर्षित करती है, लेकिन वस्तु स्थिर हो तो गुरुत्वाकर्षण गायब नहीं होता। मेज़ पर रखी किताब पर भी गुरुत्वाकर्षण लगता है; मेज़ उसे संपर्क सहारा भी देती है।

इससे एक और महत्वपूर्ण बात समझ आती है: एक ही वस्तु पर एक साथ कई बल काम कर सकते हैं और वे सभी एक ही श्रेणी के हों, यह जरूरी नहीं।

इसलिए निर्णय नियम याद रखें: परस्पर क्रिया करने वाली वस्तुएँ पहचानें, पूछें कि सीधा संपर्क जरूरी है या नहीं, फिर बल का वर्गीकरण और प्रकार बताएं। संपर्क: पेशीय बल, घर्षण। असंपर्क: चुंबकीय, स्थिरवैद्युत, गुरुत्वाकर्षण। उत्तर रटकर नहीं, प्रमाण देकर समझाएँ।”

## 5. Application worksheet — “School-day force detective”

Student task:
find six real interactions and complete:

| Interaction | Objects | Direct touch required? | Contact/non-contact | Force type | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Required mix:
- 2 contact examples,
- 1 friction example,
- 1 non-contact example,
- 1 example with more than one force acting,
- 1 example the learner initially found difficult to classify.

Reflection questions:

1. Which example was easiest to classify, and why?
2. Which example contained more than one force?
3. Why is “I cannot see the force” not enough evidence to call something non-contact?
4. Give one example where an object is stationary but a non-contact force still acts.

Hindi worksheet uses the same structure with bilingual terminology from the controlled glossary.
