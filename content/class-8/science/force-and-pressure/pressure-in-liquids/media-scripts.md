## 1. Concept video — “Depth changes liquid pressure”

Target: 5–6 minutes

### Scene 1 — Hook (0:00–0:35)

Show a transparent bottle with three side holes at different heights, all temporarily taped.

Question on screen:
**Which stream will go farthest? / कौन-सी धार सबसे दूर जाएगी?**

Reveal water streams after tape removal.

English narration:
“The lower hole often sends water farther. The important clue is depth below the water surface.”

Hindi narration:
“नीचे वाला छेद अक्सर पानी को अधिक दूर भेजता है। मुख्य संकेत है — पानी की सतह से गहराई।”

### Scene 2 — Liquids press on surfaces (0:35–1:35)

Visual:
- still bottle of water,
- arrows shown on bottom and side walls,
- side hole releases water.

Explain:
- liquid does not need to flow to exert pressure,
- pressure acts on walls and base.

Misconception card:
“Liquids push only downward” → **Incorrect**.

### Scene 3 — Depth comparison (1:35–2:55)

Animated bottle:
- A near surface,
- B deeper.

Show a simple depth scale.

Explain:
“For the same liquid under similar conditions, greater depth means greater liquid pressure.”

Do not introduce `ρgh` in this Class 8 core lesson.

### Scene 4 — Fair investigation (2:55–4:05)

Show three holes:
- similar size,
- same side,
- opened together.

Overlay:
**Control other factors. Change depth. Observe stream.**

Show a deliberately blocked/smaller hole as a bad-test example.

Narration:
“A surprising result does not automatically prove the idea wrong. First check whether your test was fair.”

### Scene 5 — Real life (4:05–5:15)

Visuals:
- dam cross-section thicker near base,
- water tank with high/low outlets,
- diver descending.

Explain depth-pressure connection.

### Scene 6 — Check understanding (5:15–5:45)

Question:
“A still tank of water is not flowing. Does it still exert pressure on its walls?”

Pause.

Reveal: **Yes.**

### Accessibility

- English and Hindi captions.
- Full transcripts.
- Narrate all diagrams/arrows.
- Do not communicate depth only through colour.
- Use large labels for “surface”, “higher point”, “deeper point”.

## 2. Practical video — “Three-hole bottle investigation”

Target: 4–5 minutes

Safety: **GREEN learner observation; adult/teacher prepares holes.**

### Safety card

Students must not use needles, blades, nails heated over flame or other sharp/hot tools to create holes.

Teacher/adult prepares a clear plastic bottle with three similar small holes before filming/activity.

### Materials

- clear plastic bottle,
- water,
- three prepared side holes,
- removable tape,
- tray/bucket/outdoor surface,
- optional ruler.

### Procedure

1. Tape all three holes.
2. Fill water above highest hole.
3. Position bottle securely.
4. Remove tapes together.
5. Observe initial stream paths.

### Learner prompts

- Which stream starts strongest?
- Which stream travels farthest initially?
- Which hole is deepest below surface?
- Were hole sizes similar?

### Explanation

For the same water and a fair setup, the deeper hole experiences greater pressure and usually produces a farther-reaching stream.

### Science-quality insert

Show three fair-test conditions:

- similar hole size,
- same liquid,
- similar opening time.

Then show one “bad test” with one hole partly blocked.

Message:
**Evidence is strongest when other factors are controlled.**

## 3. Revision audio — English

Target: 2.5–3 minutes

“Pressure in liquids becomes easy when you remember two ideas.

First, a liquid exerts pressure on the surfaces it touches. Water inside a container presses not only on the bottom but also on the side walls. The water does not have to be flowing. Even still water can exert pressure.

Second, in the same liquid under similar conditions, pressure increases with depth. A point deeper below the liquid surface experiences greater liquid pressure than a point closer to the surface.

That is why a simple three-hole bottle can be useful. If the holes are about the same size and are opened together, the lower hole often sends water farther because it is deeper below the water surface.

But a good scientist also checks the experiment. If one hole is blocked or much larger, you cannot confidently compare the streams. Control other factors before drawing a conclusion.

Do not use container width as the main rule. In this lesson, compare depth below the liquid surface in the same liquid.

The idea appears in real life too. Lower parts of dams must withstand greater water pressure. A diver experiences greater surrounding water pressure as depth increases. Lower outlets in a tank can have greater pressure than higher outlets under similar conditions.

So revise it in one sentence: liquids exert pressure on the surfaces they touch, and for the same liquid, greater depth means greater pressure.”

## 5. Application worksheet — “Depth-pressure detective”

Learner tasks:

### A. Bottle diagram

Label three holes A, B, C from highest to lowest.

For each hole predict:
- relative depth,
- relative pressure,
- expected stream distance.

Then compare with observation.

### B. Fair-test audit

Given four experimental setups, identify which one is fair and why.

Variables to inspect:
- hole size,
- liquid type,
- depth,
- opening time.

### C. Real-life transfer

Explain in one or two sentences:

1. why a dam is stronger near its base,
2. why a diver experiences more pressure deeper down,
3. why a lower water-tank outlet can have greater pressure.

### D. Misconception repair

Correct:

- “Still water has no pressure.”
- “Liquids push only downward.”
- “A wider container always means greater pressure.”

Hindi version mirrors the same tasks with controlled glossary terminology.
