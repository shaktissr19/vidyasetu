## 1. Concept video — “Air pushes too”

Target: 5–6 minutes

### Scene 1 — Hook (0:00–0:35)

Show a lightweight plastic cup filled with room-temperature water. Place a stiff card over the opening, invert over a tray and remove the supporting hand.

On-screen question:
**Why does the card stay? / कार्ड क्यों टिकता है?**

Do not reveal the answer immediately.

### Scene 2 — Air is matter (0:35–1:25)

Visuals:
- inflated balloon,
- sealed syringe with plunger resistance,
- simple particle animation.

English narration:
“Air is invisible, but it occupies space and its particles collide with surfaces. Those collisions create pressure.”

Hindi narration:
“वायु दिखाई नहीं देती, लेकिन वह स्थान घेरती है और उसके कण सतहों से टकराते हैं। यही टक्कर दाब उत्पन्न करती है।”

Misconception card:
**Invisible ≠ no pressure / दिखाई न देना ≠ दाब न होना**

### Scene 3 — Atmosphere and directions (1:25–2:20)

Show Earth surrounded by a simple atmospheric layer.

Then zoom to a box with pressure arrows pointing upward, downward and sideways.

Explain:
- atmosphere surrounds Earth,
- surrounding air exerts atmospheric pressure,
- air pressure acts in all directions.

### Scene 4 — Pressure difference, not magic suction (2:20–3:45)

Three short sequences:

1. straw in drink,
2. suction cup on smooth tile,
3. syringe without a needle.

For each, show two labels:
**Higher pressure** and **Lower pressure**.

Animate a net arrow toward the lower-pressure region.

Narration:
“Many effects we call suction are better explained as a pressure difference producing a net push.”

### Scene 5 — Return to card-under-cup (3:45–4:45)

Diagram:
- downward arrow for water/card weight,
- upward arrow for outside atmospheric pressure,
- note that sealing matters.

Important wording:
“The demonstration contains several forces. It is evidence that the pressure difference can provide enough upward support to keep the card in place.”

Do not claim that pressure is the only force present.

### Scene 6 — Leak test (4:45–5:20)

Show a poor seal. Air enters and the card falls.

Prompt:
**What changed?**

Answer:
The pressure conditions changed.

### Scene 7 — Check understanding (5:20–5:50)

Question:
“A suction cup falls from a rough wall but sticks to a smooth tile. What is the most likely reason?”

Pause.

Reveal:
**Better seal → pressure difference lasts longer.**

### Accessibility

- English and Hindi captions.
- Full bilingual transcript.
- Narrate every pressure arrow and label.
- Use words/icons as well as arrows; do not depend on colour alone.
- Keep embedded text minimal so English/Hindi overlays can be swapped.

## 2. Practical video — “Plastic cup, card and suction cup”

Target: 4–5 minutes

Safety: **GREEN**.

### Safety card

Use only:
- lightweight plastic cup,
- room-temperature water,
- stiff card,
- tray/sink,
- small suction cup.

Do **not** use glass, flame, boiling water, sharp tools or pressure vessels.

### Part A — Cup and card

1. Fill plastic cup almost completely.
2. Place stiff card flat over opening.
3. Hold card and invert over tray.
4. Slowly remove hand.
5. Observe.
6. Repeat with deliberately poor seal.

Learner prompts:
- What do you observe?
- Which direction can outside air push on the card?
- Why does a leak matter?

### Part B — Suction cup comparison

Compare:
- smooth laminated/tile-like surface,
- rough surface.

Keep suction cup and pressing force as similar as practical.

Observe:
- how well it seals,
- how long it holds,
- whether air can enter around edges.

### Science-quality insert

Observation:
“The cup stayed longer on the smooth surface.”

Inference:
“The smoother surface likely produced a better seal, helping preserve a pressure difference.”

Teach the difference between **what was seen** and **why we think it happened**.

## 3. Revision audio — English

Target: 2.5–3 minutes

“Atmospheric pressure becomes easier when you remember four connected ideas.

First, air is matter. We may not see it, but air occupies space and its particles collide with surfaces. Those collisions mean air can exert pressure.

Second, Earth is surrounded by air called the atmosphere. The pressure exerted by this surrounding air is atmospheric pressure.

Third, atmospheric pressure acts in all directions. It can push downward, upward and sideways. That is why outside air can push upward on the card beneath an inverted cup.

Fourth, many effects we casually call suction are really pressure-difference effects. When pressure in one region becomes lower than pressure around it, the higher pressure can create a net push toward the lower-pressure region.

Think about a straw. When pressure inside the straw is lowered, greater pressure on the drink’s surface helps push liquid upward. Think about a suction cup. Pressing it against a smooth surface removes some air from underneath. If the seal is good, lower pressure remains under the cup while greater atmospheric pressure outside pushes it against the surface.

A leak matters. If outside air enters the low-pressure region, the pressure difference becomes smaller and the effect weakens.

So remember: air is matter, surrounding air exerts atmospheric pressure, air pressure acts in all directions, and pressure differences explain many everyday suction effects.”

“वायुमंडलीय दाब को चार जुड़े विचारों से आसानी से समझा जा सकता है।

पहला, वायु पदार्थ है। हम उसे देख नहीं पाते, लेकिन वह स्थान घेरती है और उसके कण सतहों से टकराते हैं। इसलिए वायु दाब डाल सकती है।

दूसरा, पृथ्वी को वायु की परत घेरे हुए है जिसे वायुमंडल कहते हैं। आसपास की इस वायु द्वारा डाला गया दाब वायुमंडलीय दाब है।

तीसरा, वायुमंडलीय दाब सभी दिशाओं में कार्य करता है। वह नीचे, ऊपर और बगल की ओर दाब डाल सकता है। इसलिए उल्टे कप के नीचे की बाहरी वायु कार्ड पर ऊपर की ओर दाब डाल सकती है।

चौथा, जिन घटनाओं को हम रोज़मर्रा में सक्शन कहते हैं, वे अक्सर दाब-अंतर से बेहतर समझी जाती हैं। जब किसी क्षेत्र का दाब आसपास से कम हो जाता है, तो अधिक दाब वाला क्षेत्र कम-दाब क्षेत्र की ओर शुद्ध धक्का उत्पन्न कर सकता है।

स्ट्रॉ में अंदर का दाब कम करने पर पेय की खुली सतह पर बाहर का अधिक दाब द्रव को ऊपर धकेलने में मदद करता है। सक्शन कप को चिकनी सतह पर दबाने से नीचे की कुछ वायु निकलती है। यदि सील अच्छी हो, तो नीचे कम दाब बना रह सकता है और बाहर का अधिक वायुमंडलीय दाब कप को सतह पर दबाए रखता है।

यदि वायु रिसने लगे, तो दाब-अंतर कम हो जाता है और प्रभाव कमजोर हो जाता है।

एक वाक्य में याद रखें: वायु पदार्थ है, आसपास की वायु वायुमंडलीय दाब डालती है, दाब सभी दिशाओं में कार्य करता है और दाब-अंतर कई सक्शन प्रभावों को समझाता है।”

## 5. Application worksheet — “Pressure-difference detective”

### A. Higher or lower?

For each situation label the likely higher-pressure and lower-pressure regions:

1. drinking through a straw,
2. suction cup on a tile,
3. syringe plunger pulled back with tip in water,
4. inverted cup and card.

### B. Leak challenge

For each situation predict what happens if air leaks into the low-pressure region.

Write one sentence using:
**pressure difference / दाब-अंतर**.

### C. Observation vs inference

Given statements, label each as observation or inference:

- “The card stayed for five seconds.”
- “Outside air provided upward pressure.”
- “The suction cup fell sooner on the rough surface.”
- “The rough surface allowed more air leakage.”

### D. Misconception repair

Correct these statements:

- “Invisible air cannot push.”
- “Atmospheric pressure acts only downward.”
- “Empty space pulls a suction cup.”
- “Atmospheric pressure exists only outdoors.”

### E. Real-life explain-it

Choose one:
- straw,
- suction hook,
- medicine dropper,
- syringe without needle.

Explain the effect in 3–4 sentences without using “magic suction” as the explanation.

Hindi worksheet mirrors the same tasks using the controlled glossary terms `वायुमंडलीय दाब`, `दाब-अंतर`, `अवलोकन` and `निष्कर्ष`.
